STUDIO GUIDELINES
Topic: Depiction of Real, Living Persons
Applies to: all productions, all territories

Summary of standing policy (not tied to one production):

1. A living person may be depicted by name or clear likeness in a factual
   context (a real historical event they were actually part of) without
   consent, under newsworthiness/public-interest doctrine, PROVIDED the
   portrayal is not defamatory and does not imply a false statement of fact.

2. A living person may NOT be depicted in a fabricated scene implying they
   said or did something they did not, without a release or a clear,
   unmistakable satire/parody framing recognized in the jurisdiction of
   distribution — several distribution territories (notably several EU
   member states) do not recognize a US-style parody defense as broadly as
   US courts do.

3. Background/incidental appearance of a real person in archival or
   documentary-style footage is treated the same as brand incidental-use
   (see the "Late Autumn" clearance memo): non-promotional, non-central,
   non-defamatory background appearance is lower risk than a named,
   central, fabricated portrayal.

4. When in doubt, the default posture is: request a release. A release is
   always the lowest-risk outcome; the doctrines above are fallback
   positions for when a release genuinely cannot be obtained in time.

This is general guidance, not a substitute for case-by-case legal review —
territory-specific rules (jurisdictions field on each claim) can override
the general position above.
